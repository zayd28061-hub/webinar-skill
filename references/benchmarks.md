> **Read the difference before comparing yourself.** Corporate B2B webinars report 40-60% attendance. Live coaching offers report closer to 25%. They are different animals, and most people quote the B2B number at themselves and conclude they are broken when they are normal.

## The economics model

Work backwards. Given a target number of clients:

```
registrations_needed = clients / (show_rate × close_rate)
```

Model at **25% show** for live coaching and **7% close** unless they have their own data. So one client needs roughly **57 registrations**. Ten clients needs ~570. Run this before anyone talks about traffic spend, because it is usually the moment the plan changes.

If they are buying registrations at $42-78, that is **$2,400-4,400 of ad spend per client** at benchmark rates. If the offer is $2,000, the webinar does not work on cold paid traffic yet and the honest answer is to fix the rates or run it warm first.

## Diagnosis order — fix in this sequence, never out of it

1. **Show rate below 25%?** Nothing else matters yet. They are lost in the gap between registering and the broadcast, not in the room. Day-of reminders alone account for 38% of live attendance; a third of registrations arrive on the day itself; a text 15 minutes before recovers 8-12%.
2. **Show rate fine, close rate below 5%?** The room is full enough. The deck is the problem. Look at three slides specifically: who it is for said early, the first seven days, and whether the price was defended instead of stated.
3. **Close rate fine, attendee-to-call below 10%?** They watch and then nothing happens. That gap lives between the call to action and the calendar, not in the content.
4. **All rates at or above benchmark?** The constraint is volume, not conversion. This is the point at which spending on traffic makes sense, and not one minute before.

## The reminder sequence

The gap between intention and action is the whole reason this exists. Someone registers on Monday for a Wednesday event; by Wednesday they have forgotten, or something came up, or the interest cooled.

Six messages:
1. **Immediate confirmation** — restate the specific promise and the time, with a calendar link (+12-18%)
2. **24 hours before** — one thing they will be able to do afterwards
3. **Morning of** — 33% of registrations arrive on this day, so this email is also doing acquisition
4. **1 hour before** — the link, nothing else
5. **15 minutes before, by SMS** — recovers 8-12% of at-risk attendees
6. **Going live now**

Every message reinforces why it matters and what they will learn. None of them says "just a reminder".

## The follow-up

Half of all attendees watch on demand rather than live, and most people never build this at all.

1. **2 hours after** — one question: was it the price or the first week? Reply with either word.
2. **Next day** — the replay, with a timestamp for the part that matters
3. **Day 3** — the client closest to where they are, and what their week one looked like
4. **Day 5** — the real deadline and the real reason for it
5. **Day 7** — last one. If it is not the right time, that is genuinely fine.
